        Robot

        Inside is under the frame, toward the field center. Outside is away from the center and past the ends of the leg. Both are guesses until the team tests them: drive next to a leg and enter how close is safe. 0 turns that side off; hitting the leg itself is always flagged.

        Time estimate

        Speed limits match MecanumDrive.PARAMS (maxWheelVel 50, maxProfileAccel 50, minProfileAccel -30, maxAngVel π). Shoot (Shoot_Auto()) waits until the flywheels are at the ready RPM, feeds for Feed_Launch_ms (1.5 s), then requeues for Feed_Requeue_ms (0.75 s). Spin-up is the time from Shooter on until the flywheels reach the ready RPM; measure it on the robot (the code gives up waiting after Spinup_Timeout_ms = 2 s). Camera aim turns the robot the rest of the way to the CELL before Shoot, using the AprilTags. It only works if the planned heading is close enough for the camera to see the CELL (the “fixes up to” angle); measure both on the robot once the command exists.

        Driving the virtual robot

        Field image (optional)

        Load a top-down picture of the field (cropped to the inside of the walls, audience at the bottom). It is drawn under the path.

        Drive the robot

        WASD or arrows move, Q/E turn, hold Shift for slow. Or drag the robot; drag the dot in front of it to turn. Double-click the field to jump the robot there. Click the field first so it has keyboard focus.

        Snap to wall (the arrow pad, or Alt/Option + arrow) slides the robot straight until it touches that wall. Within 5° of square, it squares the heading too. Press two for a corner. If the robot is on the start or on the selected waypoint, that moves with it. To fix a start that is close but not touching, click the Start row (the robot jumps onto the start), then snap.

        Build the routine

        Enter adds a waypoint where the robot is. R/X shooter (flywheels) on/off, Space shoot, I/O intake on/off, T wait, F turns the robot so the shooter points at our upward CELL.

        Aim happens in two parts. F sets the planned heading, which only has to get the CELL into the camera's view. Then each Shoot with camera aim checked turns the rest of the way using the AprilTags, right before feeding. The robot code only aims at a CELL that is facing up (it checks the AprilTag roll), so if the HIVE tips during AUTO, camera aim goes for the new upward CELL, as long as the camera can see it from the planned heading. The page plans for the upward CELL you pick above.

        Intakes and motors

        The intakes are on both sides, so the robot picks up balls while it strafes. With the intake on, the side intake that leads the drive glows. L (Intake lead) turns the robot so a side intake faces the way it drives from the last pose to here; then press Enter for a new waypoint or U to update the selected one. A move with the intake on that is mostly not intake-first gets a note.

        The Motors box on the field shows what is running: the shooter, the intake, and the feeder, which is one motor running toward the shooters during a shot or backward to pull balls in. Shoot stops the feeder when it is done, even if the intake is still on, so add Intake on after a shot to keep collecting.

        Check tip

        C adds a Check tip step. The camera looks at our HIVE and the routine splits in two: the steps after Check tip run if the HIVE has not tipped, and a second list runs if it has tipped, so you can drive around and shoot at the other CELL. Use Not tipped / Tipped above the step list to see and edit each branch. Steps before Check tip are shared by both. Put Check tip where the camera can see both CELLS of our HIVE. A routine can have one Check tip. Play runs the branch you are looking at, and the other branch is drawn faded on the field.

        Shoot runs the feeder, which pushes the balls into the flywheels. Turn the shooter on at a waypoint before the shooting spot so the flywheels are at speed when the robot arrives. If the shooter is off, Shoot spins it up first and waits. The flywheels stay on after a shot until Shooter off.

        New steps go after the selected step. Click a step to select it (the robot jumps there); U moves the selected waypoint to the robot. Drag numbered dots on the field to move waypoints. Delete removes the selected step. P plays the routine.

        Field coordinates

        Inches, origin at the field center, seen from the audience: +x toward the blue alliance wall (right), +y away from the audience. Positive angles are counterclockwise. The robot's front is its shooters, and the intakes are on both sides. Headings are Road Runner's, which on our robot point out the back, away from the shooters. So heading 0 means the shooters face the red wall, and heading 180 means they face the blue wall.

        Rules it checks

        Start (G304): touching the wall, on your side (red columns A-C, blue D-F), not in the LOADING ZONE, not touching a FLOWER. During AUTO: stays on your side (G402), stays inside the walls, avoids the HIVE frame legs (and the inside and outside keepouts around them, set in Settings) and FLOWERS. End: PARK means partly in your LOADING ZONE.

        Both alliances

        The field is rotationally symmetric, so the other alliance's routine is this one rotated 180° about the center. The Code tab writes both files.

        Field layout is from the BIOBUZZ Competition Manual TU02 figures (±1 in). The HIVE drawing is approximate and the HIVE is overhead, so it is not checked for collisions.
